Micro Ch 3 · Production and Costs

Mention the types of Returns to scale.2 marks

💡 Remember: double all the inputs. Output exactly doubles → CRS. Output more than doubles → IRS. Output less than doubles → DRS.

Exam answer

Returns to scale show what happens to output when all inputs are increased in the same proportion. This can happen only in the long run. There are three types:

  1. Increasing Returns to Scale (IRS): output increases by a larger proportion than the inputs.
  2. Constant Returns to Scale (CRS): output increases by the same proportion as the inputs.
  3. Decreasing Returns to Scale (DRS): output increases by a smaller proportion than the inputs.

What are long run costs?2 marks

💡 Remember: in the long run every input can be changed, so there are no fixed costs. There are just two long run costs: LRAC and LRMC.

Exam answer

In the long run, all inputs can be varied, so there are no fixed costs. Total cost and total variable cost are the same. The long run costs are:

  1. Long run Average Cost (LRAC): the cost per unit of output. LRAC = TC ÷ q
  2. Long run Marginal Cost (LRMC): the extra cost of producing one more unit. LRMC = TC at q1 units − TC at (q1 − 1) units

Both curves are U-shaped. LRMC cuts LRAC from below at the lowest point of LRAC.

Give the meaning of Short-run and Long-run.2 marks

💡 Remember: short run = at least one input is fixed. Long run = all inputs can be changed.

Exam answer

Short run: a period in which at least one factor of production (labour or capital) cannot be changed, so it stays fixed. To change output, the firm can change only the other factor. The factor that stays fixed is called the fixed factor, and the one the firm can change is called the variable factor.

Long run: a period in which all factors of production can be changed. To produce a different level of output, the firm can change both inputs together. So, in the long run, there is no fixed factor.

Short runLong run
At least one factor is fixedAll factors can be changed
Has a fixed factor and a variable factorNo fixed factor
There are fixed costsThere are no fixed costs

Short run and long run are not measured in days, months or years. A period is short run or long run depending only on whether all the inputs can be changed.

Name any two short run costs.2 marks

💡 Remember: there are seven short run costs: three totals (TFC, TVC, TC), three averages (AFC, AVC, SAC) and one marginal (SMC). Write any two.

Exam answer

  1. Total Fixed Cost (TFC): the cost of the fixed inputs. It stays the same whatever the output.
  2. Total Variable Cost (TVC): the cost of the variable inputs. It rises as output rises.

(Other short run costs: Total Cost, Average Fixed Cost, Average Variable Cost, Short run Average Cost and Short run Marginal Cost.)

Micro Ch 4 · Theory of the Firm under Perfect Competition

State the conditions needed for profit maximization by a firm under perfect competition.2 marks

💡 Remember the three conditions: (1) P = MC, (2) MC must be rising (not falling), (3) price must cover AVC in the short run, or AC in the long run.

Exam answer

For profit to be maximum at output q0, three conditions must hold:

  1. Price must equal marginal cost (P = MC).
  2. Marginal cost must be non-decreasing at q0 (the MC curve must not be sloping downward there).
  3. Price must cover costs: in the short run, P ≥ AVC. In the long run, P ≥ AC.

Why P = MC: as long as producing one more unit adds more to revenue (MR) than to cost (MC), profit keeps rising. So profit is highest where MR = MC. For a perfectly competitive firm MR = P, so the condition becomes P = MC.

Write the meaning of opportunity cost with example.2 marks

💡 Remember: opportunity cost = what you give up from your next best choice.

Exam answer

Meaning: the opportunity cost of an activity is the gain forgone from the second best activity. In other words, it is the cost of the next best alternative given up.

Example: suppose you have Rs 1,000 and you decide to invest it in your family business. Your other choices were:

  • keep it in the house safe: no return (0%)
  • deposit it in bank 1: 10% interest
  • deposit it in bank 2: 5% interest

The best of these other choices is bank 1. Once you invest in the family business, you lose this chance. So the opportunity cost of investing in the family business is the interest you would have earned from bank 1 (10%).

Give the meaning of shut down point.2 marks

💡 Remember: shut down point = the lowest point of AVC in the short run, and the lowest point of LRAC in the long run. Below it, the firm produces nothing.

Exam answer

In the short run, a firm keeps producing as long as the price is at least equal to the minimum AVC. So, moving down the supply curve, the last price-output combination at which the firm still produces is the minimum point of AVC, where the SMC curve cuts the AVC curve. Below this point there is no production, so it is called the short run shut down point.

In the long run, the shut down point is the minimum point of the LRAC curve, where LRMC cuts LRAC.

Mention the two determinants of a firm's supply curve.2 marks

💡 Remember: the supply curve is part of the MC curve, so anything that changes MC changes supply. The two factors are technology and input prices.

Exam answer

  1. Technological progress: new technology lets the firm produce more with the same inputs, so MC falls. The supply curve shifts to the right.
  2. Input prices: if the price of an input (for example, the wage rate) rises, MC rises. The supply curve shifts to the left. If input prices fall, it shifts right.

Give the meaning of price elasticity of supply and write its formula.2 marks

💡 Remember: elasticity of supply tells you how strongly supply reacts when the price changes. It is % change in quantity ÷ % change in price.

Exam answer

Meaning: the price elasticity of supply of a good measures how responsive the quantity supplied is to a change in the price of the good.

Formula:
es = Percentage change in quantity supplied ÷ Percentage change in price
= (ΔQ ÷ Q) × (P ÷ ΔP)

Example: the price of a cricket ball rises from Rs 10 to Rs 30, and supply rises from 200 to 1,000 balls.
% change in quantity = (800 ÷ 200) × 100 = 400
% change in price = (20 ÷ 10) × 100 = 200
es = 400 ÷ 200 = 2

A vertical supply curve has es = 0. An upward-sloping supply curve has a positive es.

Define Marginal Revenue.1 mark

Answer: Marginal Revenue (MR) is the increase in total revenue from selling one more unit of output.
MR = ΔTR ÷ Δq, or MR = TRn − TRn−1

💡 Example: 2 boxes of candles bring TR = Rs 20 and 3 boxes bring TR = Rs 30, so MR = (30 − 20) ÷ (3 − 2) = Rs 10. In perfect competition, MR always equals the price.

Micro Ch 5 · Market Equilibrium

How wage is determined in the Labour market?2 marks

💡 Remember: the wage is set where the demand for labour (from firms) meets the supply of labour (from households).

Exam answer

  • In the labour market, households supply labour and firms demand it. This is the opposite of the goods market. Here, labour means hours of work, not the number of workers.
  • Demand curve for labour slopes down: a firm hires labour up to the point where the wage equals the marginal revenue product of labour. Because marginal product falls as more labour is hired, firms hire less labour at a higher wage.
  • Supply curve of labour slopes up: at higher wages, more people are willing to work.
  • The equilibrium wage rate is set where these two curves intersect. Here, the labour households want to supply equals the labour firms want to hire.

Diagram

Define equilibrium price and quantity.2 marks

💡 Remember: equilibrium = market demand equals market supply. There is no excess demand and no excess supply.

Exam answer

  • Equilibrium price: the price at which equilibrium is reached in the market, that is, the price at which market demand equals market supply.
  • Equilibrium quantity: the quantity that is bought and sold at the equilibrium price.

In symbols: (p*, q*) is an equilibrium if qD(p*) = qS(p*). On a diagram, it is the point where the demand and supply curves cross.

💡 The "invisible hand" raises the price when there is excess demand and lowers it when there is excess supply, until the market reaches equilibrium. Adam Smith is the founding father of modern economics.

Micro Ch 2 · Theory of Consumer Behaviour

Mention two approaches which explain consumer behaviour.2 marks

💡 Remember: Cardinal = utility in numbers. Ordinal = utility in order (ranks).

Exam answer

  1. Cardinal Utility Analysis: assumes utility can be measured in numbers. For example, "this shirt gives me 50 units of utility".
  2. Ordinal Utility Analysis: assumes utility cannot be measured in numbers. The consumer can only rank bundles as more or less preferred. It uses indifference curves.
What is monotonic preference?2 marks

💡 Remember: monotonic = "more is better". If one bundle has more of at least one good and no less of the other, the consumer prefers it.

Exam answer

A consumer's preferences are monotonic if, between any two bundles, she prefers the bundle that has more of at least one of the goods and no less of the other good.

Example: (10 bananas, 10 mangoes) is preferred to (10 bananas, 9 mangoes), because it has one more mango and the same number of bananas.

So, between any two indifference curves, the bundles on the higher curve are preferred to the bundles on the lower curve.

State the Law of Demand.2 marks

💡 Remember: price ↑ → demand ↓. Price ↓ → demand ↑. Price and demand move in opposite directions.

Exam answer

The Law of Demand states that, other things being equal, there is a negative relationship between the demand for a commodity and its price. When the price of the commodity rises, its demand falls, and when the price falls, its demand rises, other factors remaining the same.

Why it happens: because of diminishing marginal utility, each extra unit gives less satisfaction, so the consumer buys more only at a lower price.

What do you mean by Price Elasticity of demand? Write its formula.2 marks

💡 Remember: eD = % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price.

Exam answer

Meaning: price elasticity of demand measures how responsive the demand for a good is to a change in its price.

Formula:
eD = Percentage change in demand for the good ÷ Percentage change in the price of the good
= (ΔQ ÷ Q) × (P ÷ ΔP)

Example: the price of bananas rises from Rs 5 to Rs 7 (+40%) and demand falls from 15 to 12 (−20%). eD = −20 ÷ 40 = −0.5, so |eD| = 0.5 and demand is inelastic.
